# Phase 2b: fixed frequencies, Pareto frontiers and an I/O ablation (modelled)

**Scope: MODELLED energy and MODELLED completion time, SIMULATED frequency.** Nothing in
this folder is measured. No real energy savings and no novelty are claimed. Where our
policy is dominated or loses, that is reported.

Generated by `python -m benchmark.pareto --outdir evidence/phase2b_pareto` at the clean
commit in `PROVENANCE.json`, using the unchanged closed-loop model of
`benchmark/closed_loop.py` (see `evidence/phase2a_closed_loop/README.md`). `SHA256SUMS`
covers every generated file plus `PROVENANCE.json` (not this README or itself); the folder
is `-text` in `.gitattributes`. `tests/test_pareto.py` regenerates the CSVs and
`summary.md` byte for byte.

## Files

| `frontier.csv` | Per scenario, fraction and policy: mean energy and time, on the frontier or not, and for dynamic policies the comparison with fixed frequencies |

## Assumptions (same model as Phase 2a)

- Work units of CPU work, I/O wait and idle time from the synthetic profiles; CPU time
  scales as s + (1 - s) x fmax / f; I/O and idle time do not depend on frequency.
- One simulated CPU, 1 s decisions, existing classifier, Policy and power model
  (P = 21.6 + 32.4 x (f/5.1 GHz)^3 x util/100 W). Ladder 3.8 to 5.1 GHz only.
- Pareto comparisons use the mean of 20 seeds per scenario. Dominance: lower or equal mean
  energy and mean time, with at least one strictly lower.

## Frontier

- **All four fixed frequencies are on the frontier in every scenario and every s (36 of
  36 cells each).** In this model, a fixed frequency is always a reasonable trade-off.
- Dynamic policies are on the frontier in some cells only: fast_up_slow_down 34/36,
  schedutil_style_ioboost 29, ours_k3_rampdown 27, ours_k1 27, adaptive_k 26,
  schedutil_style 25, ours_k3 22, conservative 22, ours_k5 17. (performance and powersave
  are identical to static 5.1 and 3.8 GHz.) ours_k3's 22 include the 4 flapping cells
  where it never switches and equals static 5.1 GHz.

## Does our policy beat a fixed frequency?

Not reliably. **A fixed frequency dominates ours_k3 in 9 of 36 cells**: static 4.667 GHz in
archived_seed11 (s = 0, +18.7 J, +1.33 %), archived_plan_seed23 (s = 0 and 0.3, up to
+1.55 %), high_noise_seed31 (all four s, +1.40 to +1.82 %, up to +23.9 J); static 4.233 GHz
in cpu_bursts_on_idle_seed51 (s = 0 and 0.3, +0.72 % and +0.34 %). "Dominates" means the
fixed frequency used that much less energy without taking longer. In the other 23 cells
ours_k3 uses 0.03 % to 8.9 % less modelled energy than the best fixed level that is no
slower (usually static 5.1 GHz; 4.667 GHz in io_heavy and 4.233 GHz in cpu_bursts). In
flapping_cpu_idle it is identical to static 5.1 GHz. Every dynamic policy other than
performance and powersave (which equal fixed levels), including the schedutil-style
approximation, is dominated by some fixed level in at least 2 cells (summary.md lists all).

## I/O ablation: does lowering frequency during I/O help?

Our K=3 policy with the I/O-class target fraction 1.0 (never lower), 0.6, 0.3 (default) or
0.0 (lowest level), and the mixed-class fraction 1.0 or 0.6 (default):

- **io_heavy, s = 0 (pure 1/f):** lowering during I/O cuts energy but costs time. With
  mixed 1.0: io 1.0 = 1936.6 J / 65.00 s, io 0.6 = 1890.4 J / 66.31 s, io 0.3 = 1856.2 J /
  67.91 s, io 0.0 = 1834.9 J / 69.95 s. EDP is lowest at io 0.6 (125360 J·s) and highest
  at io 0.0 (128358 J·s): aggressive lowering hurts EDP.
- **io_heavy, s >= 0.3:** every step of lowering improves both energy and EDP (for example
  s = 0.6, mixed 0.6: EDP 125284 at io 1.0 down to 115717 at io 0.0), because the CPU
  share of I/O phases slows down less.
- **cpu_bursts_on_idle_seed51:** the I/O and mixed fractions make no difference at all.
  That scenario has no I/O or mixed phases, so it cannot answer the question.

So in this model, whether lowering frequency during I/O helps depends on how much of the
CPU work in I/O phases is frequency-insensitive. The model cannot tell us which value is
realistic.

## What would settle it

Native Linux on real hardware with cpufreq control (on the Ryzen 7 7840HS, amd-pstate in a
mode that accepts OS targets or bounds), lower P-states than 3.8 GHz, measured package
energy (RAPL counters or an external power meter), and real bounded workloads with known
I/O and CPU phases, repeated with each policy and each fixed frequency, measuring both
energy and completion time. None of this is available on the VirtualBox VM.

## Limits on interpretation

- Modelled, not measured; synthetic workloads; one simulated CPU.
- The 21.6 W static term dominates the power model, which favours lower frequencies on
  energy and makes fixed levels hard to beat.
- The ladder has only four levels, all high; real savings come from lower P-states.
- schedutil_style is an approximation, not the Linux schedutil governor.
